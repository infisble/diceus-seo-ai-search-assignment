Learn more ERP made plain Enterprise resource planning (ERP) is a staple of the professional ecosystem for most high-tech companies today. Having evolved from the original MRP—a material resources planning tool responsible for supplying the organization with necessary resources for smooth functioning—modern ERP systems serve as a one-stop shop with a shared database covering most of the firm’s business operations. ERP’s various modules cater to the needs of a company’s employees enterprise-wide (from manufacturing departments’ workers to sales and marketing teams) by streamlining and facilitating production, financial management , order fulfillment, procurement and inventory management, workforce administration, warehouse management, marketing, and more. Although ERP software is quite versatile and multifunctional, it can’t do everything independently. To become an ultimate tool, it should work well with other internal solutions and third-party software programs the company employs in its shop floor routines. This is where ERP system integration comes into play. What is ERP integration? A contemporary commercial organization leverages a broad assortment of various IT products in its pipeline activities, among which ERP is the backbone. Some are legacy systems, while others are cutting-edge SaaS solutions. Some are cloud-based tools, whereas others belong to on-premises software. Some are off-the-shelf products, but others are custom-made. Each relies on its own database and architecture, which sometimes excludes their coherent usage and business data exchange. ERP integration refers to integrating this system into the enterprise’s wider digital environment and providing its smooth interaction with all other components, as well as third-party apps and data sources. As a result, organizations obtain a unified ecosystem with a centralized database comprising compatible and consistent products that are visible to users and operate in harmony. What boons does ERP integration usher in? Benefits of ERP integration Companies enjoy the following perks when they connect ERP systems with other software. ERP integration benefits Pinch and spread for zoom Single source of truth One of the scourges modern enterprises suffer from is data silos. It prevents personnel in, say, customer service departments from accessing the information available to sales teams and vice versa. ERP integration helps create a unified and consistent repository of accurate data, thus promoting seamless data flow and eliminating discrepancies that cause misunderstandings and errors. Real-time data Cloud ERP systems connected with other modules allow for real-time updating of vital customer and financial data. It guarantees that managers and employees have an up-to-date picture of events and business operations to adopt knowledgeable decisions and collaborate effectively in providing the best-in-class customer experience. Besides, real-time data analytics is a surefire recipe for staying ahead of the curve and getting a sharp competitive edge. Minimizing manual data entry When the ecosystem’s elements function in isolation, the staff has to enter the same data into multiple applications. This sheer waste of time and effort that often generates errors is avoided thanks to organization-wide data integration as a part of ERP integration. Now, when one employee feeds some information into any module, it automatically appears in relevant IT components without a single keystroke from others. Making legacy software last longer Upgrades and replacements are big-ticket and time-consuming endeavors that may seem impractical and even needless, while older systems work well. By integrating them with ERP, you can continue using them and avoid major disruption to business continuity by letting pipeline processes flow seamlessly along established lines. Scalability and flexibility Business growth spells more complex operations and more intricate business rules. Integrated ERP software enables companies to add new elements and scale integrations of other modules, keeping abreast of the organization’s evolution and introducing upgrades into current workflows. A comprehensive view of customers CRM/ERP integration (joining other client-facing products that operate customer data into a single network) allows organizations to obtain a 360-degree view of their clientele. This holistic picture painted by people’s purchasing history and all interactions with the brand is a source of insights into customer demographics, needs, preferences, and pain points, enabling companies to shape their advertising and marketing strategies and personalize customer experience to the maximum. Vulnerability management When organizations largely automate business processes and minimize human workforce involvement due to multiple ERP integrations, they address the lion’s share of vulnerabilities related to software security risks, operational inefficiencies caused by manual processes, miscommunications, and other inadequacies in the shop floor pipeline. Streamlined employee training When you integrate data access through the ERP system alone, you don’t need to teach the workforce to use various solutions. They will just have to learn how to leverage ERP to handle data related to any internal workflow. Faster ROI All the previously mentioned perks come together to result in a quicker return on investment than would happen with disparate enterprise IT products. Businesses integrate processes within their shop floor routine under one digital roof, thus boosting productivity, efficiency, analytics power, and customer satisfaction across the entire organization. ERP integration methods Pinch and spread for zoom Point-to-point integration Establishing direct links between two systems using custom code is the most straightforward, quickest, and most cost-effective integration approach. It is just what the doctor ordered for small and medium-sized organizations whose IT environments have a couple of components to unite. However, it is not the best option for companies poised for expansion. As the number of integrations increases, the point-to-point model becomes unwieldy, time—and effort-consuming. Besides, it is a manual endeavor dependent on human coding and implementation, which makes it slow in execution and prone to inaccuracies. Middleware-based integration Leveraged for complex integrations involving on-premises architecture, this method relies on the middle layer of translation called the enterprise service bus (ESB). This centralized middleware component orchestrates intricate interactions between connected systems without altering them. It allows for the correct transformation and routing of data by translating specific data formats relevant to certain software types to and from the standard XML language. While perfect for vertical legacy systems, this approach doesn’t fit horizontal cloud-based architectures of SaaS solutions. Since it doesn’t support intelligent automation, ESB usage (like the point-to-point model) requires much manual effort and technical expertise to set up and implement integration flows. API-based integration This strategy presupposes utilizing well-defined protocols that connect ERP with other digital infrastructure components. API-based integration has the definite advantages of being high-speed and more reliable. The downsides include its unsuitability for event-based architecture and hybrid data flows and the need for manual support. So, while choosing an integration technique, you should weigh its pros and cons and consider each method’s pitfalls. Zooming in on the common ERP integration challenges Knowing the ERP creation lifecycle’s ins and outs perfectly, DICEUS is well aware of the bottlenecks its integration will likely encounter. Data translation Records kept in different departments may be in various formats. The same is true of data coming from solutions of disparate nature and origin (legacy, bespoke, niche, etc.). Making them compatible with each other and the ERP system requires extra steps and specialized technical competence from the team. Integration platform as a service (iPaaS) tools contain prebuilt connectors that dramatically facilitate data translation. Lack of expertise and resources ERP integration is a big-ticket item that skilled professionals should implement. For many modest businesses, such financial and expertise demands may prove forbidding. Internal resistance As ERP integration ushers in, some team leaders or even whole departments may not relish the idea of their data or workflows’ extended visibility. The organization’s management should do their best to explain the advantages of the integrated IT ecosystem and create a universal buy-in among the personnel. Disruption of business operations Like any large-scale endeavor, ERP integration takes time. The staff also needs some time to learn the new solution. To reduce both and flatten the learning curve, you should have a meticulous integration strategy that will allow the company to keep business continuity disruption to a minimum. Security risks Data handling operations always pose the risk of sensitive information exposure or leakage. That is why, while planning and implementing ERP integration, you should follow the best data security practices and ensure regulatory compliance of the integrated system. Frequently asked questions What is ERP integration, and why is it important? ERP integration is the procedure of connecting the ERP system that an organization relies on in its work with other components of the enterprise’s digital environment. By synchronizing their operations and joining them with a common database, companies receive an efficient, seamless IT ecosystem that addresses all their business needs and facilitates major workflows. What benefits can businesses achieve through ERP integration? When you integrate ERP with other enterprise software, you create a unified repository of accurate and consistent business data updated in real-time and a collection of easy-to-master, scalable, and flexible tools that provide a holistic view of customers, minimize manual data handling, ensure top-notch vulnerability management, and guarantee faster return on investment. What are the different types of ERP integration methods? The three basic models for ERP integration are point-to-point integration, middleware-based integration, and API-based integration. The choice of method is conditioned by the number of solutions to be connected, their architecture, IT personnel expertise, and the expected scalability of the unified system. What are the future trends in ERP integration technology? Today, we can witness the growing demand for connecting ERP systems with mobile platforms and IoT devices, and the increasing role of AI and ML in boosting the efficiency of such combined environments. In the future, ERP integration will be taken over by an integration platform as a service (iPaaS) tool, a simple, no-coding instrument ideal for working with cloud-based products.
