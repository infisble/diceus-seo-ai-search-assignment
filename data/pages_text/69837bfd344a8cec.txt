Discover our AI consulting services Generative AI as large as life The name of the technology is indicative of its overarching goal. It is geared for synthetic data generation in various formats (documents, audio, video, images, software code) after being trained on the existing data. The main requirement for the outcome generative AI creates is to resemble the user input information as much as possible. How do generative AI models work? At their core, generative AI mechanisms are deep neural networks specializing in certain tasks and relying on statistical algorithms. The first group of them, called transformers, peruses training data fed into the system and classifies it according to pre-defined criteria. Then, variational auto-encoders (VAEs) come into play. They encode the raw data by translating it into compressed code and pass it on to two generative adversarial networks (GANs). The first – generator network – is honed to identify patterns across original datasets and produce new data points in line with the pinpointed characteristics. While it happens, the second network, known as the discriminator, constantly compares the input and the output to determine how much the latter is similar to the former. Finally, VAEs are activated again to turn the encoded generated data back into the necessary format for users to enjoy. Operating along the described lines, generative AI software is employed across multiple verticals (among which are education, design, retail, logistics, IT, insurance, healthcare, entertainment, and others), where it excels at content creation, video game development, improving the quality of medical imaging, drug discovery, crafting advertisements, personalizing marketing efforts, improving underwriting accuracy, learning material production, software development, making virtual product displays, supporting customer service, and more. It is easy to guess that predictive AI makes predictions about future events. Solutions leveraging predictive AI serve this purpose by analyzing historical data patterns and revealing trends or anomalies to forecast future outcomes of some actions or predict possible developments in the market. How do predictive AI models work? At first, AI engineers and data scientists collect all available data relevant to a specific field or a concrete task like predicting customer behavior or optimizing supply chain operations. Then, the assembled data is preprocessed since predictive AI relies heavily on the quality of input information to guarantee the accuracy of predictive analysis. To ensure the topmost data quality, it is curated to contain only relevant, complete, understandable, up-to-date, and consistent data points compatible with the predictive models you employ. After that, the data collection is divided into training and testing sets, where the first is utilized to train the model, whereas the second helps developers to evaluate the predictive AI’s ability to forecast market trends or predict future outcomes. With the data prepared, the model is trained via employing complex algorithms leveraged in the machine learning domain (neural networks, decision trees, linear regression, etc.) and conditioned by the data availability and nature as well as by the type of predictive analytics you want to obtain. This is an iterative process during which the model learns patterns, minimizes the difference between the actual values the training set contains and the expected outputs, and continuously adjusts its parameters when an error or discrepancy is detected. As soon as the model’s accuracy is found satisfactory, it can be committed to solve real-life tasks. Today, predictive AI solutions find application in a wide range of industries (banking, retail, healthcare, manufacturing, e-commerce, entertainment, public sector, sports, you name it) where they conduct financial forecasting, perform fraud detection, optimize inventory and supply chain management, exercise predictive maintenance of machinery, improve diagnostics, enhance player scouting procedures, support proactive urban planning, increase population safety, manage resource allocation, and more. Key differences between generative AI and predictive AI The contrast between predictive AI vs generative AI is made evident when these technologies are juxtaposed along several basic parameters. Objective Generative AI focuses on producing original data or new content (audio, video, images, texts, code), while predictive AI aims to analyze existing records to predict future developments. Learning process Historical data serves predictive AI as a collection of insights for forecasts. Generative AI is taught to recognize relationships and patterns across multiple data points. Training data To perform its function, predictive AI requires historical data, whereas generative AI doesn’t limit itself to historical records only, drawing upon diverse and comprehensive datasets. Training complexity Generally, predictive AI models are easier to train, while the training process utilized for generative AI solutions is more complex and resource-consuming. Algorithms employed Predictive AI’s reliance on statistical algorithms and machine learning models (clustering, time series, decision trees, regression models, etc.) enables it to analyze data and make predictions. Generative AI algorithms are more complex (such as transformer models, GANs, VAEs, and diffusion models), involving deep learning to generate data similar to the original input. Use cases Generative AI is honed for synthetic data generation and content creation. Predictive AI applications evolve around business analytics and financial forecasting. Creativity Predictive AI doesn’t produce anything new; it only reveals regularities spotted across datasets. Generative AI rivals human creativity in crafting realistic images, videos, or original texts. Challenges Being limited to existing patterns, predictive AI solutions are blind to novel scenarios. Generative AI’s output may lack specificity sometimes, so it requires quality control. Given the exposed differences, we can offer our recommendations as to the choice of an appropriate technology. Frequently asked questions What is the difference between generative AI and predictive AI? Generative AI solutions rely on complex algorithms and large datasets to create new content in various formats (from texts and software code to audio and videos). Predictive AI tools utilize statistical methods and machine learning techniques to sift through historical data and expose patterns across it, which are then used as valuable insights for predicting future developments. What are the main applications of generative AI? Generative AI tools are leveraged in education, design, IT, healthcare, entertainment, retail, insurance, and other industries for software development, content creation, drug discovery, crafting learning materials, making advertisements and visual product displays, empowering customer service with chatbots and virtual assistants, personalizing marketing campaigns, and more. How does predictive AI improve business decision-making? Predictive AI tools analyze tons of historical data and reveal underlying trends, patterns, and anomalies. Using the outcomes as valuable insights, C-suite managers can accurately predict market fluctuations and consumer demand dynamics, which allows them to adopt knowledgeable operational and strategic decisions. What are the limitations of predictive AI in forecasting? You should bear in mind that forecasting always presupposes a lack of certainty. Moreover, to be of any use, prediction initiatives require huge volumes of high-quality historical data. If such records lack relevance, consistency, timeliness, conformity, integrity, completeness, or accuracy, even the best-in-class predictive AI software will produce imprecise or ambiguous outcomes. How can businesses choose between generative AI and predictive AI solutions? The major tipping factor is the application purpose. Generative AI tools excel at creating new content, whereas predictive AI solutions are geared toward business intelligence and analytics. Other considerations a company should pay attention to while making a choice include training data availability, computational resources it has access to, and the project’s scalability needs.
