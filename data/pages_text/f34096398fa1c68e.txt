Core capabilities Executive dashboard Open claims, new FNOL, total reserved, total paid, net loss, average cycle time, SLA compliance, fraud rate, and litigation rate, filterable by period, line of business, and segment. Financial overview Total reserves, total paid, outstanding exposure, recovery collected, net incurred, and loss ratio, broken down by line of business and by claim segments, for example, Fast Track, Standard, Complex, CAT, or Litigation. Adjuster performance Claims per adjuster, average cycle time, SLA compliance, reserve accuracy, reopen rate, and escalation rate, with side-by-side adjuster comparison and score. Advanced analytics Build a filter on any claim field (severity, LOB, status, and more), save it as a view, visualize the result, and turn a recurring filter into a rule. Reimbursement analytics Total reimbursement claims, total exposure, average deductible size, and approval rate, broken down by underlying insurers. Configured per client Dashboards, KPI thresholds, claim segments, and available filters are set up to fit your line of business and program structure. Leakage and recovery Settlements above reserve, recovery identified against recovery collected, and subrogation opportunities that were never pursued, by segment and by adjuster. Fraud and anomaly detection Claims frequency analysis and anomaly patterns across the register, with suspicious claims flagged for review. Payments, recoveries, and reimbursements update paid totals, net incurred, and loss ratio. Book a demo FAQ We already run claims through another system. Can this analytics module sit on top of it? The dashboards are built on the claims data inside our Claims Management module. If claims are managed elsewhere today, moving the register or, at minimum, reproducing the fields shown here, such as FNOL, reserves, adjuster assignment, and reimbursement, is what we scope during implementation. We’re a small company with a limited number of claims a year. Is this overbuilt for us? No. The dashboards and filter builder work the same at low claim volumes; only the configuration changes. A program with a handful of open claims still benefits from seeing reserves, SLA status, and reimbursement in one register instead of a spreadsheet. We built our own claims tracking spreadsheet or BI report. Why move off it? A spreadsheet or a BI report built on an export is accurate on the day it’s built and starts drifting the moment someone edits the source file outside it. These dashboards read the same register adjusters and TPAs update directly, so dashboards stay current without a separate refresh step. Our claims are handled by a TPA. Does the analytics still work for us? Yes. Reimbursement Claims and Reimbursement Analytics were built for exactly this structure – tracking deductible reimbursement and underlying insurer recoveries by program, separate from the primary claim record, which is how most companies run through a TPA. We only need one or two dashboards, not the whole set. Can we start there? Yes. Each dashboard reads from the same register, so it’s possible to configure and roll out one view first and add the others later without rebuilding the data model. Does this replace our BI tool? No, and it is not meant to. Your BI tool reports across the business; this reports on the claims register, from inside the system the register lives in. Teams that keep both use these dashboards for day-to-day claims decisions and send the same data onward for company-wide reporting. How do these dashboards help with claims leakage? Leakage appears as settlements above reserve, recoveries identified but never collected, and payments outside the segment’s normal range. The views put all of those in the same place as the claim they belong to, so they are displayed while the claim is open rather than at quarter end. Which lines of business does it cover? All of them, because the structure is configured. Lines of business, claim segments, and KPI thresholds are set up during implementation, which is why a motor book and a medical stop-loss programme can run the same dashboards with different definitions underneath.
